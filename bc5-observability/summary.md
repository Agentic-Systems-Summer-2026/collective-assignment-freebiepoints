# why long-running agents need checkpoints

When agents crash or get interrupted during long tasks, they typically lose all progress made up to that point, forcing them to restart from the beginning and waste valuable time and resources. Checkpointing solves this problem by periodically saving the agent's complete state—including variables, memory, and execution position—creating recovery points that allow the agent to resume from the most recent checkpoint instead of starting over. Agents cannot simply resume from their last known state without explicit checkpoints because they may have been in the middle of complex operations, memory changes, or network interactions that are difficult to reverse reliably. Therefore, checkpointing provides a crucial safety net that protects against data loss and maintains productivity in extended workflows.
